Before you begin, each player must prepare the following:

  • One Driver card
  • One Vehicle card
  • One personal deck of exactly 40 cards

Driver & Vehicle

  • Each player chooses one Driver and one Vehicle and places them face-up in their respective zones.
  • Once the game begins, your Driver and Vehicle do not change for the duration of that game.

Deckbuilding Rules

  • Your deck must contain exactly 40 cards.
  • Your deck can be a mix of Fuel, Items, and Utilities in any ratio you choose.
  • There are no restrictions on how many cards of each rarity you include overall, but there are restrictions on duplicates of the same card:
    • Common (green): up to 4 copies of the same card
    • Uncommon (yellow): up to 3 copies of the same card
    • Rare (purple): up to 2 copies of the same card
    • Mythical (blue): only 1 copy of a given card
    Example: you may only have one Bullet Bill of a specific version, but you could have both Blue Shell and Spiny Shell because they are different cards.

Starting the Game

  • Thoroughly shuffle each player’s deck.
  • Each player draws a starting hand of 7 cards.
  • Your hand may go above 7 during the turn, but at the end of your turn you must discard down to 7.
  • Turn order is decided by each player rolling a d20. Highest roll goes first, then play proceeds clockwise.
  • Players agree on the finish line (the number of positions required to win), and all players begin at position 0.
  • Any card in your opening hand that states it must be played immediately (for example, Starting Boost or Thundercloud) is played before the game officially begins. Any ongoing passive or delayed effects start from that point.

Each player’s turn follows this structure:

  1. Start-of-Turn Effects
    Resolve any effects that say “At the start of your turn…”.
  2. Draw
    Draw one card from the top of your deck. If this card states it must be played immediately, do so before continuing.
  3. Untap Fuel
    Untap (stand upright) any Fuel cards you tapped on previous turns. If you have no Fuel in your Fuel Zone, skip this step.
  4. Place Fuel
    You may place one Fuel card from your hand into your Fuel Zone (unless a Utility or effect allows you to place more).
    • You cannot place Fuel if there are already 8 Fuel cards.
    • Cards like Superfuel may provide multiple fuel points but still only occupy one of the 8 Fuel card slots.
  5. Movement Roll
    Roll a d6 to determine your base movement. Your total movement is:
    d6 result + Driver Speed + Vehicle Speed + relevant passive modifiers.
    Apply all relevant passive effects as you calculate this movement.
  6. Update Position
    Add your total movement to your current position on the track.
  7. Main Action Window
    In any order:
    • Place Items into your primary and/or pocket slots (respecting any fuel costs to place if applicable).
    • Use Utilities, as long as you have sufficient Fuel to pay any tap/burn costs.
    • Activate Driver abilities by burning Fuel, if you have enough Fuel cards in your Fuel Zone.
    • You may burn Fuel to discard an Item card in your primary slot that you do not wish to use, moving your pocket item up:
      • Common (green) discard: free
      • Uncommon (yellow) discard: burn 1 Fuel card
      • Rare (purple) discard: burn 2 Fuel cards
      • Mythical (blue) discard: burn 3 Fuel cards
    • You may place at most two Items at a time:
      • The first Item is placed face-up as your primary item.
      • The second Item may be placed face-down as your pocket item.
    • An Item placed this turn usually cannot be used until your next turn (unless a card explicitly says otherwise).
    • When you use your primary Item, discard it. Your pocket Item is then flipped face-up and becomes the new primary Item, leaving a free pocket slot.
    • Items have two abilities (offensive and defensive). You may use one or the other when you play them, never both.
    • All Items and Utilities used go to your discard pile after resolving.
    • Use any applicable passive abilities from your Vehicle card.
  8. End of Turn
    If your hand exceeds 7 cards, discard down to 7. Then formally end your turn so play passes to the next player.

The game uses several distinct areas (“zones”) that define where cards are and how they interact.

  • Deck – A face-down pile of exactly 40 cards. Cards are drawn from the top unless a card effect states otherwise.
  • Hand – Cards drawn from your deck that you may choose to play. Cards in hand cannot be used defensively.
  • Driver Zone – Holds your chosen Driver card. It remains face-up for the entire game.
  • Vehicle Zone – Holds your chosen Vehicle card. It remains face-up and provides passive abilities.
  • Fuel Zone – Where you place Fuel cards. You may have up to 8 Fuel cards here, though total fuel points may exceed 8. Fuel can be tapped and burnt from this zone.
  • Primary Item Slot – The face-up Item ready to be used first.
  • Pocket Item Slot – The face-down reserve Item. When your primary is used or discarded, your pocket flips face-up and becomes the new primary Item.
  • Discard Pile – A face-up pile where used Items, used Utilities, burnt Fuel and other discarded cards go. You may inspect your own discard pile any time.
  • Position Track – The track (or representation of it) showing each player’s current position in the race. All movement, knock-backs, and win conditions reference this track.

The following terms are used consistently throughout the rules and on cards. They have fixed meanings.

  • Tap – Rotate a Fuel card sideways to show it has been used this turn. Tapped Fuel cannot be used again until it untaps at the start of your next turn.
  • Burn – Permanently discard a Fuel card from your Fuel Zone. Burning is usually required for Driver abilities or to discard certain high-rarity Items.
  • Fuel Points – The usable amount of fuel a card provides when tapped or burnt. Some cards grant multiple fuel points.
  • Primary Item – The face-up Item in your primary slot that must be used before your pocket Item becomes active.
  • Pocket Item – The face-down Item stored in your pocket slot. Once the primary Item is used or discarded, the pocket Item is flipped and becomes the new primary Item.
  • Passive Ability – An ability that is always active while the card is in play. In MKS: TCG, passive abilities belong to Vehicle cards.
  • Trigger – An ability that activates when a specific condition is met (e.g., “When you dodge an attack…”).
  • Activated Ability – An ability that requires a cost to use. For Drivers this cost is always burning Fuel. For Utilities and other cards, this may involve tapping or burning Fuel.
  • Hit Threshold – The number an attacking Item needs to meet or exceed in order to successfully hit a defender who rolls a dodge.
  • Undodgeable – An Item or effect that cannot be avoided with a dodge roll. Other forms of defence may still apply (e.g., reflection or suppression).
  • Suppress – Negates an effect entirely as though it never occurred.
  • Reflect – Redirects the final effect of an Item or ability to a different legal target. Reflected effects may be reflected again unless stated otherwise.
  • Override – Replaces the normal outcome of an Item or ability with a new stated outcome.

To keep games fair and clear, abilities and effects are grouped into specific timing windows.

Start-of-Turn Window

  • Resolve all effects that say “At the start of your turn…”.
  • Any automatically played cards that trigger at the start of your turn also resolve here.

Movement Window

  • Roll your d6 for movement.
  • Apply Speed from your Driver and Vehicle, plus relevant passive modifiers.
  • Update your position on the track.

Main Action Window

  • Place Fuel (if you have not already done so this turn).
  • Play Utilities (paying any required Fuel costs).
  • Activate Driver abilities by burning Fuel.
  • Place Items into your primary and/or pocket Item slots.

End-of-Turn Window

  • Discard down to 7 cards if you have more.
  • Formally end your turn.

Reaction Window (Outside of Turn)

  • Triggered when another player takes an action that targets you or affects the table (usually Items or certain Utilities).
  • During this window, you may defend yourself using:
    • Your primary Item (or pocket Item once it becomes primary)
    • Driver abilities that explicitly state they can be used in response
    • Utilities that the table has agreed may be used reactively
    • A dodge roll (d20 + your total Handling), if the Item is dodgeable
  • You cannot place new Fuel during this window.

Driver abilities in MKS: TCG are always activated abilities that require burning Fuel. Drivers do not have passive or purely triggered abilities.

  • Each Driver has one or more abilities that show a Fuel burn cost.
  • To use a Driver ability, you must burn (discard) that many Fuel cards from your Fuel Zone.
  • Driver abilities normally can only be activated during your Main Action Window.
  • Some Driver abilities will explicitly state they can be used in response to other actions (for example, to block, dodge, suppress, ignore, copy, or override an effect). These may be used during the Reaction Window when their conditions are met.

All passive effects belong to Vehicles. Any “always on” abilities on Drivers should be treated as misprints and clarified by the project team if they appear.

You cannot freely play cards during another player’s turn. You may only act in response to something happening that allows a reaction.

  • You may use Items to defend yourself if:
    • They are already placed as your primary Item, or
    • Your pocket Item becomes the primary Item after you use/discard your original primary.
  • You cannot use Items from your hand as defence. They must be placed on the table first.
  • For a dodge attempt, you roll a d20 + your total Handling stat. Whether this succeeds depends on the Item’s hit threshold. Some Items are undodgeable.
  • Certain Driver abilities dealing with blocking, dodging, suppressing, ignoring, copying, or overriding effects may be used defensively if the card text allows it.
  • Fuel cannot be placed during another player’s turn, and passive Vehicle abilities do not change timing: they remain passive and continuous.

When an Item is used, follow this process unless the card states otherwise.

  1. Declare the Item
    Choose which ability you are using (offensive or defensive). You may only use one of the two abilities.
  2. Choose Targets
    Declare which player(s) or object(s) are being targeted. If multiple players are equally valid (for example, tied for closest or farthest), the selection method (e.g. coin flip) should follow the card text or a mutually agreed house rule.
  3. Allow Responses
    Other players may now respond with defensive Items, Utilities, or Driver abilities that are allowed during the Reaction Window.
  4. Resolve Dodge Rolls
    If the Item is dodgeable, the targeted player may roll a d20 and add their total Handling. Compare this to the Item’s hit threshold to see if the attack lands.
  5. Apply Effects
    Apply the Item’s results, taking into account any suppressions, reflections, overrides, or other modifications that have been declared.
  6. Discard the Item
    After resolution, place the used Item into its owner’s discard pile.

If the user’s position changes during resolution (for example due to reflection or some other effect), continue resolving the Item based on their new position unless the card specifically prevents this.

Reaching the finish line is not an instant win. A player must survive a full round at or beyond the finishing position.

  • When your position first reaches or exceeds the agreed finishing threshold, place your marker at the finish.
  • The game continues until it is your turn again.
  • At the start of your next turn, if you are still at or beyond the finishing threshold, you win the race.
  • If at any point before your next turn you are knocked back below the finishing threshold, you have not won yet. You must move back to or beyond the finish line and then again reach the start of your next turn there.
  • Multiple players may be at or beyond the finish line at the same time. The player who first completes a full turn at or beyond the threshold wins.

After a winner is declared, the game can end or you can continue playing to determine second, third, and further positions.

Some situations are intentionally left open to house rulings so each playgroup can choose how chaotic or precise they want their games to be.

Negative Positions

If a player would be moved to a position below zero, agree as a table how to handle this. Some common variants:

  • Burnout – Position cannot go below 0. Any further negative movement is ignored.
  • False Start – Position cannot go below 0, but excess negative movement ‘rolls over’ into penalties (for example, losing Fuel or suffering debuffs).
  • Handicap / Pity Bill – Position cannot go below 0, and additional negative movement instead grants a compensating effect (e.g., temporary Speed or Handling buffs).
  • Bagging – Allow positions to go into negative numbers. Players may choose to fall far behind to benefit from powerful catch-up Items later.

Tied Positions

Many Items or Utilities may reference “the player in X position” or “the furthest behind player”. When multiple players are tied at that position, you may:

  • Agree that the current player chooses among tied players, or
  • Decide via random method (e.g., coin flip, highest d20 roll among tied players).

As long as the method is agreed and consistent, it is valid.

Utilities cover a broad range of effects. Understanding their general roles helps with deckbuilding and play.

  • Boost Utilities – Increase your movement or adjust your position on the track.
  • Fuel Utilities – Manipulate Fuel (adding, untapping, burning in specific ways, etc.).
  • Defensive Utilities – Block, reduce, reflect, or suppress incoming attacks or effects.
  • Item Utilities – Swap Items, steal Items, move Items between slots, or alter how Items behave.
  • Position Utilities – Move you or other players forwards or backwards or interact directly with the track and positions.
  • Meta Utilities – Draw extra cards, search your deck, change your hand size, manipulate the discard pile, etc.

A single Utility may fall into more than one category. When in doubt, the specific text on the card always overrides any general behaviour described here.

When multiple effects interact or conflict, use the following order to determine what happens.

  1. Suppression
    If an effect is suppressed, it is treated as if it never occurred. Suppression resolves before other modifications.
  2. Override
    If an ability overrides an effect, replace the original result with the new one specified by the overriding effect.
  3. Reflection
    If an effect is reflected, redirect the final outcome of the effect to a new legal target. The reflected effect may itself be reflected again if allowed.
  4. Normal Resolution
    If no suppression, override, or reflection applies, resolve the effect as written on the card.

When multiple abilities or effects are trying to resolve at the same time:

  • The active player chooses the order in which their own simultaneous effects resolve.
  • Then, in turn order, each other player chooses the order in which their effects resolve.

Sometimes multiple abilities will trigger at the same time (for example, when several players cross certain thresholds or play similar effects).

  • First, the active player resolves all of their triggered abilities in any order they choose.
  • Then, moving clockwise, each other player resolves their triggered abilities, also choosing the order for their own triggers.
  • If an effect would target one of several players equally (e.g., tied positions), use whatever tie-breaking method your table has agreed on (usually a random method like a coin flip or d20).

This structure prevents timing deadlocks and keeps the game flowing even in chaotic situations.

Rarity in MKS: TCG is a guideline for how impactful a card tends to be, but it does not automatically make a card unbeatable.

  • Higher-rarity cards (Rare, Mythical) generally have stronger or more complex effects than Common and Uncommon cards.
  • However, a Common card can absolutely shut down or counter a higher-rarity card in the right circumstances.
  • Some Utilities and Items have “universal” effects — for example, a reflector-style card that can reflect any level of Item, including Mythicals.
  • Deckbuilding, timing, and clever play matter more than simply stacking all your highest-rarity cards.

The design philosophy is that higher rarity typically offers more flexibility, explosiveness, or consistency, but it never guarantees victory on its own.
